FPAM Compliance Policy and Operational Guidelines for Public Assets Maintenance

Public Assets Maintenance Manual

Comprehensive compliance and operational guidelines issued by the Federal Public Assets Maintenance (FPAM) Department of the Federal Ministry of Housing and Urban Development. Applies to all MDAs that occupy, manage, or operate public buildings, facilities, and assets across Nigeria.

FPAM Department

Maintenance Categories
Two categories define how maintenance is handled across all federal assets
Routine / Minor Maintenance
Cleaning, painting, pest control
Basic plumbing and electrical repairs
Replacement of consumables (bulbs, filters, locks)
Handled internally by MDA maintenance units without FPAM notification required.
Major Maintenance
Structural works — roof, foundation, walls
Mechanical/electrical system upgrades (HVAC, power, water)
Renovations, refurbishments, and modernisation projects
Safety compliance upgrades (fire systems, accessibility)
Must be reported to FPAM. FPAM supervises to ensure quality, transparency, and accountability.
Reporting Protocol
5-step process for major maintenance reporting and execution
1
Initial Report
MDA maintenance unit identifies a major issue and submits a Maintenance Request Form via FPAM's online portal or official channel.
MDA Action
2
FPAM Assessment
FPAM conducts inspection — onsite or digital verification — to evaluate the scale, cost, and urgency of the required maintenance.
FPAM Action
3
Approval & Funding
FPAM approves the works, allocates budget if centrally funded, or guides the MDA on procurement procedures.
FPAM Action
4
Execution
FPAM deploys specialised contractors and technical staff in collaboration with the MDA's internal maintenance unit.
FPAM + MDA
5
Certification & Handover
FPAM certifies work completion, issues the Facility Management Clearance Certificate, and updates the National Asset Maintenance Register.

User Guidelines for Assets Maintenance
Guidelines for all occupants and civil servants using public buildings and facilities
Proper Use of Public Facilities
Maintain Cleanliness
Dispose of all trash in designated bins. Keep areas tidy. Visitors play a key role in maintaining cleanliness — dispose properly, keep restrooms clean, and clean up after meetings or events.
Report Issues
Identify the location and type of damage (broken windows, cracks, leaks). Take photos where possible. Determine and contact the responsible authority promptly. Follow up if not addressed within a reasonable timeframe.
Follow Capacity Limits
Adhere to occupancy limits in office spaces, lifts, car parks, and common areas. Overcrowding causes safety risks and damage to facilities.
Cleanliness Policies
No smoking, no pets, no spitting. Be mindful of food and drinks in restricted areas. Respect others' personal space and property.
Strict Gender-Specific Conveniences
Use conveniences strictly according to their designated gender purpose. Keep restrooms clean — flush after use, wash hands, dispose of hygiene products in designated bins.
Waste Management
Dispose of waste according to classification — plastics, papers, metals separately. Use designated recycling bins where available. Support the building's waste management plan.
Security, Safety & Surveillance
Do
Follow safety guidelines and emergency procedures
Install and maintain security cameras and alarms
Train guards and staff to report suspicious activities
Keep building entrances and exits well-lit
Have an emergency response plan in place
Regularly check and maintain fire safety equipment
Use emergency exits and fire extinguishers properly
Don't
Allow unauthorised access to the building
Ignore suspicious activities or threats
Leave doors or windows unlocked or propped open
Ignore safety hazards — loose wires, slippery floors
Leave valuables unsecured or unattended
Tamper with security cameras, alarms, or access control
Block walkways, doors, or emergency exits
Electrical Fittings & Appliances
Right Usage
Ensure electrical fittings are professionally installed by licensed electricians
Switch off all appliances when not in use, especially at close of work
Use power strips with surge protectors for sensitive equipment
Report any electrical faults to the maintenance department immediately
Wrong Usage
Overloading circuits by plugging too many devices into one socket
Using appliances with exposed wires or broken plugs
Daisy-chaining multiple extension cords — fire hazard
Using electrical appliances near water sources with wet hands
Organisational Roles & Responsibilities
Who does what in public asset maintenance
FPAM Department (FMHUD)
Sets maintenance standards, maintains the national asset database, coordinates capacity building across all MDAs, supervises major maintenance works, and ensures quality, transparency, and accountability.
MDA Maintenance Units
Handle routine and minor maintenance internally. Must be trained by FPAM on protocols for maintaining federal government buildings and monitoring maintenance tasks. Required to submit major issues to FPAM.
External Contractors
Handle specialised services including HVAC, electrical, plumbing, and landscaping. Must be FPAM-certified. Only FPAM-certified personnel are authorised to handle public building maintenance.
Chief Resident Architect (CRA)
Certifies completion of monthly facility management works. Signs off on the Facility Management Clearance Certificate alongside the Facility Manager after each reporting period.
Maintenance Planning & Strategy
All strategies must be supervised, coordinated, and overseen by FPAM
Preventive Maintenance
Regular routine checks on key systems. A proactive strategy that involves scheduled inspections, servicing, and minor repairs to prevent major breakdowns, extend asset lifespan, and improve operational efficiency.
Condition Surveys
Every 5 years per National Building Codes. Comprehensive surveys covering building envelope, lighting systems, HVAC, electrical systems, and water usage. Building surveys require maintenance plans for all new federal public building structures.
Reactive Maintenance
Emergency repairs responding to unexpected failures. Should be minimised through effective preventive measures. Must be reported to FPAM when categorised as major maintenance.
Lifecycle Costing
Decision-making framework for repair vs replacement. Integrate maintenance budgets into all new projects. Allocate funds for preventive maintenance to reduce long-term costs. Use competitive procurement for outsourced services.

Sustainability & Technology Integration
Modern standards for efficient, sustainable public asset management
Energy-Efficient Systems
Use energy-efficient systems, solar power where feasible, and water-saving fixtures. Conduct energy audits covering building envelope, lighting, HVAC, electrical systems, and water usage.
IoT & Digital Monitoring
Apply IoT sensors for real-time monitoring of building systems — temperature, humidity, power consumption, and security. AssetSpatial serves as the central digital registry for this monitoring infrastructure.
Green Building Principles
Implement green building principles in all renovation and refurbishment projects. Waste must be disposed according to classification — plastics, papers, metals separately.
Training & Certification
FPAM shall develop a National Maintenance Training Program (NMTP). Training conducted quarterly with certification. Only FPAM-certified personnel are authorised to handle public building maintenance.

Enforcement of the National Public Buildings Maintenance Order, 2022
Federal Ministry of Housing and Urban Development · Office of the Permanent Secretary
This order expressly empowers FPAM to coordinate, monitor, and enforce compliance with the maintenance of public buildings and assets, ensuring that these national investments are preserved for long-term functionality, safety, and efficiency.

Reference: Federal Republic of Nigeria Official Gazette No. 84. Dated 4th May, 2022, Vol. 109 pgs. B1087–B1089, Government Notice No. 120 on the National Public Buildings Maintenance Order, 2022.
Signed — Dr. Shuaib M.L Belgore, CON, NPOM · Permanent Secretary, FMHUD
MDA Compliance Obligations — All Ministries, Departments & Agencies Must:
1
Designate a Maintenance Desk Officer
Appoint a dedicated officer within your Ministry/Department/Agency to liaise directly with FPAM on all maintenance matters.
2
Develop & Submit a Maintenance Schedule
Develop and submit a comprehensive Maintenance Schedule covering all physical assets under your care, in accordance with FPAM's guidelines.
3
Budget for Maintenance Activities
Include maintenance budgets in annual submissions to ensure adequate funding for implementation and preventive upkeep.
4
Grant Access for Compliance Inspections
Grant FPAM access, when required, to assess the state of public infrastructure under your agency and ensure adherence to national maintenance standards. FPAM will carry out periodic inspections and compliance reviews.
